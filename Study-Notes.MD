Before formal learning begins, learners need a **baseline understanding** of AI concepts, expectations, and potential risks. This module ensures everyone enters the course with shared foundational awareness.

## 🔍 Pre-Course Survey (Highly Expanded)

The purpose of this survey is to understand learner backgrounds, expectations, and psychological readiness.

### **Sample Questions (With Purpose Notes)**

1. **"Have you used ChatGPT, Gemini, or any AI tool before? Describe your experience."**
   *Assesses familiarity and identifies overconfidence or misconceptions.*

2. **"What do you hope to gain from this course?"**
   *Aligns content with learner motivation (career, curiosity, productivity, etc.).*

3. **"Rate your confidence in AI literacy (1–10)."**
   *Provides a measurable benchmark to compare against post-course growth.*

4. **"What concerns do you have about AI?"**
   *Reveals anxiety points such as job loss, ethics, misinformation, privacy.*

5. **"Do you believe AI can think or have emotions? Why or why not?"**
   *Surfaces common misconceptions about AI sentience.*

6. **"How often do you rely on recommendations from platforms like TikTok, Netflix, or Grab?"**
   *Introduces the idea that AI is already omnipresent in daily decisions.*

## 🧠 Reflection Prompt (Case Study — Expanded)

### **Case:**

A marketing executive uses generative AI tools to speed up content creation. They paste **sensitive customer segmentation data** into an online AI tool without checking privacy settings.

The AI model stores the input for model improvement, unintentionally exposing private information.

### **Reflection Questions**

* What went wrong in this scenario?
* Which assumptions did the executive make about AI safety?
* What risks emerged from misunderstanding how data is used?
* What basic AI literacy knowledge could have prevented this?
* What *company policies* should have been in place?

### **Learning Outcome**

Learners recognize that:

* AI literacy isn’t optional.
* Misuse can lead to **data leaks, compliance breaches, or reputational damage**.
* Even powerful AI tools require **responsible and informed usage**.

# **Module 1 — What *Is* AI? (Deep Expanded Notes)**

## 💡 Definition of AI

Artificial Intelligence refers to **systems designed to perform tasks that would normally require human intelligence**.

Examples of “human” tasks AI can mimic:

* Understanding language
* Recognizing patterns
* Making predictions
* Reasoning and planning

---

## 🤖 Examples of AI in Daily Use

* **Google Maps**: predicts traffic using millions of user data points.
* **Netflix**: recommends content based on viewing behavior.
* **Banks**: detect fraudulent transactions using anomaly detection.
* **Customer support**: chatbots automate first-line query handling.
* **E-commerce**: AI-driven dynamic pricing.

---

## 🧬 Types of AI (Greatly Expanded)

### **1. Artificial Narrow Intelligence (ANI)**

* Performs a *single task extremely well*.
* Most modern AI systems fall here.
* Examples:

  * ChatGPT writing text
  * Grammarly detecting grammar errors
  * Tesla Autopilot lane detection
  * Spotify music recommendation algorithms

**Case Example:** A fast-food chain uses AI to predict hourly demand and prep ingredients accordingly.

---

### **2. Artificial General Intelligence (AGI)**

* Mimics human cognitive abilities.
* Can generalize knowledge across domains.
* **Does not exist yet**, but researchers debate timeline projections (2030? 2100? Never?).

---

### **3. Artificial Superintelligence (ASI)**

* Surpasses human intelligence.
* Often portrayed in sci-fi movies.
* Hypothetical; no real system exists.

# **Module 2 — How AI Works (Deep Dive)**

## 🧠 Machine Learning (ML) Overview

Machine learning is the ability for a system to **learn from data** rather than being explicitly programmed.

### **Case Study — Spam Filtering**

AI is trained using millions of labeled emails.

* Words like "prize", "free", "bitcoin" increase spam probability.
* Legitimate sender domains decrease spam probability.

The model learns continuously — if new spam trends emerge, retraining is needed.

---

## 🧠 Neural Networks (Expanded)

A neural network consists of:

* **input layer** (features)
* **hidden layers** (pattern detection)
* **output layer** (final prediction)

### **Example: Predicting Housing Prices**

Input features:

* number of rooms
* location
* distance to MRT
* building age

Hidden layers learn complex patterns like:

* prime districts ≈ higher prices
* proximity to schools increases demand
